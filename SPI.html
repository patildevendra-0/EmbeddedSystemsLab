Think of it like two conveyor belts and a spotlight

CS (Chip Select) = the spotlight. The controller shines the spotlight on exactly one device at a time. Only the device standing in the spotlight (CS LOW) is allowed to talk — everyone else stays silent, even though they hear the same clock.
SCLK = the conductor's metronome. It ticks at a steady rate so both sides know exactly when to hand over the next bit — no bit moves except on a tick.
MOSI and MISO = two separate conveyor belts running at once — one carrying boxes from the controller to the device (MOSI), the other carrying boxes back from the device to the controller (MISO), moving on the very same ticks. That's full-duplex: both belts run together, not one after another.
Once this picture makes sense, press play and watch the same story happen on the real CS / SCLK / MOSI / MISO lines.

SPI mode — CPOL / CPHA

CPOL sets the clock's idle level (0 = idle LOW, 1 = idle HIGH). CPHA sets which clock edge data is sampled on (0 = first edge, 1 = second edge). Together they give four SPI modes — the controller and target must agree on the same mode or bits get sampled at the wrong moment.
