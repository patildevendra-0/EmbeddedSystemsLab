What's happening

Slow start: cwnd grows by roughly 1 MSS per ACK, doubling per round trip, to quickly probe available capacity.

Congestion avoidance: once cwnd passes ssthresh, growth slows to roughly 1 MSS per round trip.

On timeout: ssthresh drops to half of cwnd, and cwnd resets to 1 MSS — a conservative restart.

On fast retransmit (3 duplicate ACKs): ssthresh halves and cwnd jumps directly to the new ssthresh, skipping a full slow start.

This is a simplified conceptual model — real stacks (Reno, CUBIC, BBR, …) differ in their exact growth functions.

Guided Scenarios

Each one runs a real sequence through the same simulator

Normal connection

Handshake, data transfer, graceful close — nothing goes wrong.

Packet loss

A data segment is dropped; watch duplicate ACKs trigger fast retransmit.

High latency

RTT jumps to ~400ms — see how it slows the handshake and ramp-up.

Out of order

Segments are reordered in the network; the receiver reports a gap.

Slow receiver

The server stops reading; watch rwnd shrink toward zero window.

Congestion

Heavy loss simulates a congested path; cwnd repeatedly collapses.

Wrong port

Client connects to a port nobody is listening on — server responds RST.

Reset

An established connection is abruptly reset instead of closed gracefully.

Zero window

Server's receive buffer fills completely; sender must pause and probe.

Graceful close

Full four-way FIN/ACK teardown with independent half-closes.

TCP vs UDP

Same network, two very different contracts

TCP

  • Connection-oriented (handshake required)
  • Reliable, ordered byte stream
  • Acknowledgements + retransmission
  • Flow control (rwnd)
  • Congestion control (cwnd)
  • Higher overhead, more predictable delivery

UDP

  • Connectionless — no handshake
  • Datagrams, no reliability guarantee
  • No acknowledgement or retransmission
  • No flow control
  • No congestion control
  • Lower overhead, lower and more predictable latency per packet
