I²C Interactive Lab

Watch a controller find, address, and exchange data with devices on a shared two-wire bus.

Think of it like a classroom roll-call

Controller (MCU) = teacher. The teacher starts roll-call (START) and claps a steady rhythm (SCL = clock) so everyone knows when to listen or answer.
Each device = a student with their own name — that name is the device's address (e.g. Sensor = 0x48).
SDA = the shared microphone. The teacher calls a name into it. Every student hears it, but only the student whose name matches answers — "Present!" — that answer is the ACK.
Data exchange happens next, one word (bit) per clap of the rhythm.
STOP = teacher says "that's all", the microphone goes quiet and idle again (pulled back up by the pull-up resistor, like the mic returning to its stand).
Once this picture makes sense, press play below and watch the exact same story happen on real SDA/SCL wires.
SDA = the microphone
One shared wire everyone talks on — names, "Present!", and data all travel here, one at a time.
SCL = the clapping rhythm
Doesn't carry words — just claps steadily so everyone knows exactly when to read what's on SDA.
